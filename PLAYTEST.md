# Family playtest guide

Short, separate sessions for each child. The goal is to find the most confusing step and fix it, not to
prove the game "works". Nothing here is recorded or uploaded.

## Before you start

- Use the device they'll normally play on (tablet preferred), in landscape, with sound on at a
  comfortable level.
- In the grown-up area, check each child's starting help level (More help or More exploring). You can
  change it any time.
- Have paper ready for notes. Don't film or record.

## Each session (10–15 minutes per child)

1. **Invite neutrally.** Say "Try this game." Don't explain the controls. If they ask, try "What do you
   think you could do?" before helping.
2. **Watch quietly.** Note when they:
   - act on their own (what did they tap first?)
   - hesitate (where, and for how long?)
   - experiment (do the same thing differently on purpose)
   - smile or laugh (at what?)
   - ask for help (what did they ask?)
   - lose interest (what was on screen?)
3. **Write down the exact confusing step.** Then decide which cause fits best:
   - **Control**: they knew what to do but couldn't do it (a target too small, a drag too fiddly).
   - **Instruction**: they didn't know what to do (the demonstration or caption was unclear).
   - **Pacing**: they knew, but it took too long or too many steps.
   - **Objective**: they didn't know why to do it.
4. **Ask two simple questions afterwards:** "What was your favourite thing?" and "Was anything tricky?"
   Don't lead them toward praise.
5. **Try stopping.** Ask them to press the moon button (Save and finish). Later, open the game again and
   check that their kite tail is in the clubhouse and they return to the same place.
6. **Change one thing.** Pick the most concrete usability or play problem, change it (or tell the
   developer), and try again another day.

## Things worth noticing (not scoring)

- Do they find Pip and try the launcher, the windmill, or both?
- What do they do at the "not yet" moment: wait, wave again, wander off?
- When the wagon rolls into the ribbon, which option do they pick, if any? Do they want to fix it?
- Do they use the help (lightbulb) button? Did the demonstration hand make sense?
- Can they stop when asked, and come back happily later?

Please don't treat long sessions, repeated launching, or difficulty stopping as signs of success. And
enjoying the game doesn't show that real-world social skills changed. Those are things to notice
outside the game, gently, over time.
